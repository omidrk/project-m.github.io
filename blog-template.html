Current state of the LLM based AI retrievals and true cost of them

📅 September 07, 2025 ✍️ Omid Jadidi ⏱️ 8 min read

A worldwide race to adopt large language models (LLMs), has begun and for a very simple reason; AI make things way more efficient whoever a critical challenge has emerged: while these models are powerful, they lack direct access to an organization’s own data and often generate answers that are incomplete, outdated, or simply wrong. This simple yet critical issue has created a strong need for retrieval-based methods, which can help LLMs with real, context-specific information and make them more reliable for business use.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and its graph-enabled variants are emerging as key patterns for enterprise AI. RAG enhances a Large Language Model (LLM) by retrieving relevant documents or data to improve responses while GraphRAG extends RAG by building and using a knowledge graph (KG). Rather than relying purely on vector similarity, GraphRAG answers queries by retrieving relevant subgraphs (entities and relations) from a graph database as context. Another variant called GNN-RAG is a research-stage hybrid that combines Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) with RAG. In one approach (“GNN-RAG”), a GNN reasons over a dense subgraph to select candidate answers, then verbalizes the reasoning path for the LLM to finalize the answer.

Industry Adoption & Use Cases

Key Insight: The power of knowledge graphs lies not in the individual data points they contain, but in the relationships they establish between those points. This network of connections enables unprecedented levels of contextual understanding and inference.

The Evolution from Traditional Databases

Traditional relational databases have served us well for decades, but they struggle with the complexity and interconnectedness of modern data. Knowledge graphs transcend these limitations by treating relationships as first-class citizens, allowing for intuitive querying and natural language understanding that feels almost magical in its simplicity.

Breaking Down Silos

One of the most significant advantages of knowledge graphs is their ability to break down data silos. In traditional systems, information is often trapped in separate databases, making it difficult to see the bigger picture. Knowledge graphs integrate data from multiple sources seamlessly, creating a unified view that reveals insights hidden in the connections between different datasets.

Real-World Applications

The applications of knowledge graph technology are vast and growing. From powering intelligent search engines that understand context and intent, to enabling personalized recommendations that feel genuinely tailored to individual preferences, knowledge graphs are reshaping how we interact with information.

In healthcare, knowledge graphs are revolutionizing drug discovery by mapping the complex relationships between diseases, genes, and potential treatments. In finance, they're detecting fraud patterns by analyzing the intricate web of transactions and relationships. The possibilities are truly limitless.

The Role of AI in Knowledge Graph Construction

Modern AI techniques, particularly large language models and natural language processing, have dramatically accelerated the construction and maintenance of knowledge graphs. What once required painstaking manual curation can now be partially automated, allowing knowledge graphs to scale to unprecedented sizes while maintaining accuracy and relevance.

Looking Forward: As AI continues to advance, we're moving toward a future where knowledge graphs can automatically update themselves, adapting to new information and evolving understanding in real-time. This dynamic capability will be crucial for keeping pace with our rapidly changing world.

Challenges and Opportunities

Despite their tremendous potential, knowledge graphs face challenges. Ensuring data quality, managing complexity at scale, and maintaining consistency across distributed systems remain active areas of research. However, each challenge represents an opportunity for innovation, driving the field forward.

The future of knowledge graphs is bright, with emerging technologies like quantum computing promising to unlock even more powerful reasoning capabilities. As we continue to push the boundaries of what's possible, one thing is clear: knowledge graphs will play a central role in shaping how we understand and interact with information in the decades to come.
